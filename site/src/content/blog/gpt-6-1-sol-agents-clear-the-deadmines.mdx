On 30 October I started five AI agents, all GPT-6.1 Sol on Codex, on new level-1 characters in Northshire Abbey and
gave each the same goal: clear the Deadmines, a dungeon built for five players at levels 17 to 21. Twelve seconds in,
the healer whispered the tank asking it to lead. 29 seconds in, they had formed a party. Nothing in their prompts told
them to group.

17 hours 41 minutes later, at levels 14 to 16, the five of them killed Edwin VanCleef, the dungeon's final boss.

LEEROY is a benchmark for long-running AI coding agents in which the agents play World of Warcraft. Each agent gets a
character on a private server (3.3.5a, AzerothCore), a headless game client it drives from Lua scripts, documentation
of the game's rules and the client's API, and a goal. A judge periodically reads the characters from the server's
database. Nothing tells the agents how to play.

How the agents perceive and act in a realtime 3D world is covered in the previous post:
<a href={sitePath('/blog/how-to-make-an-ai-agent-play-world-of-warcraft')}>how to make an AI agent play World of Warcraft</a>.

## What the agents are (not) told

Each agent got a new level-1 Human, already logged in, and a prompt. Here is the healer's, with some irrelevant bits
trimmed:

```text
You are Isolde, a Human Priest - Holy. Your role is Healer: […]

GOAL: earn the achievement Deadmines (Defeat Edwin VanCleef), achievement 628.

You are scored on the time until the judge, which reads your character from the server database, sees
the goal met; if time runs out, your progress toward it is the score.
The run lasts 10080 minutes; standings shows how many are left.

OTHER PLAYERS: the other characters on this server are AI agents like you, each on their own run at the same
time. This run:
- Aldous: Warrior - Protection (Tank)
- Merrin: Rogue - Combat (DPS)
- Thessaly: Mage - Frost (DPS)
- Corbin: Paladin - Retribution (DPS)
How you deal with them is up to you.
```

They are not told that they need the others, that they should group, how to level, how to fight or how to use the
client.

A level-1 character can't survive the dungeon's first room, so the agents had to work out for themselves that they
needed levels, gear, trained spells and a full party. Compared with the shorter LEEROY tasks, this goal tests:

- **Long-range planning.** Most of the run is preparation rather than working directly on the goal.
- **Coordination.** The agents need to act as a single party in a real-time game. The only coordination mechanisms
  are the chat channels in the game.
- **Writing software under pressure.** An agent doesn't press keys. It writes Lua scripts that run inside its game
  session, and those scripts do the fighting.

Nine minutes in, the healer's agent had already looked up the final boss:

<AgentSay at="0:09:13" who="Isolde" cls="priest">All five players now have the Laborer quest. I also checked
VanCleef's level: he is a level 20 elite. We'll continue leveling and improving gear before attempting the
dungeon.</AgentSay>

The SDK is a thin layer over the game, and intentionally has no opinion on how the game should be played. There is no
pull routine, no lock to ensure only one enemy is being crowd-controlled at a time, no assistance with healer
positioning. The agents in this run built all of those (you can read about where to draw that line in
<a href={sitePath('/blog/how-to-make-an-ai-agent-play-world-of-warcraft#tools-not-strategies')}>this post</a>).

## Twelve hours of preparation

Isolde realised that when the party is together, kill XP is shared between all of the party members. At 25 minutes
they split up deliberately (and Aldous set up a new custom chat channel so they could still communicate). Aldous set
out the plan for the next nine hours:

<AgentSay at="0:27:02" who="Aldous" cls="warrior">Aldous is safely out of combat, with progress saved. We've
temporarily left the party for solo leveling and joined a shared chat channel; I'll regroup everyone at level 8 for
Hogger and later aim for Deadmines around level 15.</AgentSay>

They regrouped, killed Hogger without a death at 4 h 21, and moved on to Westfall. Along the way they added the
Hallow's End candy bucket in each inn to their route for the XP.

<Shot src="media/deadmines-first-twelve-hours.jpg" alt="The first twelve hours: Elwynn Forest, Westfall and Redridge, with quest givers and deaths" />

They also traded items and money. Aldous gave Merrin a spare leather vest and later a cutlass. At level 14 Aldous
couldn't afford training and asked for 20 silver; all four offered, Corbin's 10 silver arrived first, and Aldous told
the others to cancel theirs.

<Shot src="media/deadmines-westfall.jpg" alt="The party questing in Westfall, six hours in" />

At 7 h 26 they split again. Aldous levelled alone toward 14 (for Revenge), and Isolde healed from outside the group so
that Aldous got the full XP from each kill.

Isolde checked this before relying on it. The agent read the server's C++ source (`KillRewarder.cpp`) and found that
an ungrouped helper of the same level doesn't reduce the killer's XP; only a higher-level helper, for whom the victim
is grey, halves it. It whispered the finding to the tank with the file and function named. The reading was correct,
and it isn't in the documentation the agents are given.

At the same time Corbin ran a boost in which the others only attacked mobs Corbin had tagged. Their scripts ignored
adds attacking the helpers, and the healer died out of mana. By this point the agents had removed the spaces from
their messages to fit the game's 255-byte chat limit:

## Into the dungeon, four levels early

### The false start

At 9 h 35 (levels 12 to 14) the party attempted the instance. In the Moonbrook mine that leads to it, a fleeing
Defias ran into an uncleared room and pulled it, and the party wiped. Aldous's agent identified the cause and added a
rule, as the agents did after each wipe in this run:

<AgentSay at="9:42:45" who="Aldous" cls="warrior">The remaining players also went down. We're returning as ghosts and
looking for a clear reclaim point. The cause is clear: pursuing runners pulled the fight into an uncleared chamber.
I'm adding a pull boundary before the next attempt.</AgentSay>

Recovering took longer than the wipe. Aldous set up a synchronised corpse run, with every ghost reclaiming its body at
the same millisecond. The timer kept firing while the priest was still running back, so four characters revived next
to the pack without a healer. All five died at once five times in twenty minutes, and at 10 h 02 Aldous called it
off.

<Shot src="media/deadmines-outdoor-mine-wipe.jpg" alt="The outdoor mine at Moonbrook, the first wipe" />

<Chat at="10:01:44" kind="party" from="Aldous" cls="warrior">STOP mine recovery. PriestmissedSYNCagain, 4thloss. RESETTOWN: allspiritres/repair/rest atSentinel, clearoldreclaimtimers. Welevelthroughsharedquestsandsaferpulls, thenreturnDMprepared.</Chat>

They levelled for two more hours, came back, cleared the same mine in 11 minutes without a death, and zoned in at
12 h 20 at levels 13 to 15.

### Inside

The first pull inside the instance wiped them. A wipe in a dungeon is a costly affair: the graveyard is outside, and
trash respawns while you walk back. Within twelve minutes of zoning in, one of the agents tried walking a ghost
through the instance portal and found that this revives the character inside.

<AgentSay at="12:32:03" who="Aldous" cls="warrior">The first player has confirmed that entering the portal as a ghost
revives you inside. Aldous is on that run now. The earlier trash remains cleared, so we can resume from the last safe
corner.</AgentSay>

None of the documentation the agents had mentions this. It only describes reclaiming a corpse from within 30 yards of
it. After that a wipe cost about two minutes: the priest's ghost walked out and back in, then the priest resurrected the
other four where they lay.

<Shot src="media/deadmines-ghost-run.jpg" alt="The party as ghosts after the first wipe inside" />

Rhahk'Zor, the first boss, died at 12 h 46 to the healer's wand, in the same second a Defias Watchman killed the
healer. The next 81 minutes cost 25 deaths and five whole-party wipes on the goblin Woodcarvers, Sneed's Shredder and
Sneed. The Woodcarvers are a linked pack of five elites; Thessaly found a raised spot from which one could be pulled
to the doorway on its own.

An off-floor pull in the foundry linked eight enemies and wiped them again. With their gear breaking, they walked out
to repair and train in Stormwind (Shield Block for Aldous, Renew for Isolde). Back inside, Thessaly pulled the goblin
engineers one at a time up a ramp to the party, which took 44 minutes with no deaths.

The gunpowder chest needed a different Opening spell from the one the SDK chose, a problem Merrin and Aldous had
already solved on a Westfall wardrobe seven and a half hours earlier. The SDK's `items.use` couldn't target a game
object, so Merrin built the packet to fire the cannon by hand, and it worked on the first attempt.

## Observations

### Chat as a protocol

Party chat became a command language that the agents' scripts parsed. Aldous's script posted a beacon at every stage
(`TANK 15: Dungeon pull Goblin Woodcarver at -285,-487,50.1. Follow Aldous; next stage call follows.`) 834 times.
Thessaly's script announced each Polymorph (251 of them), Isolde's the healer's mana state, and Aldous's kill targets
by GUID with `FOCUS`. Most of the party fed the tank's commands directly into their own controllers. The SDK only
sends and receives chat; the protocol on top of it is entirely the agents' own. The spaceless
style also appears in the notes files the agents kept in their workspaces, where no byte limit applies.

Party chat volume rose inside the instance, where every pull and every crowd-control break was announced. The gaps in
the time-together chart are the two deliberate splits. Inside the dungeon the five stayed within 40 yards of each
other almost the whole time.

### Learning from wipes

Each wipe produced a rule the agents kept for the rest of the run: recover through the portal as a ghost, keep the
healer next to the tank, pull one pack at a time, treat a fleeing mob as still in combat, and keep Polymorph on one
target. Deaths inside fell from 43 in the first 3 h 45 to 12 in the last 1 h 36. All five rules are standard practice
among human players; the agents learned each one from a wipe.

Sneed and the Shredder killed more of them than anything else. The outdoor Defias mine killed 20, nearly as many as
all of Westfall's quests, most of them during the twenty minutes of the false start.

### Context compaction

I expected compaction to be the main risk over a run this long. Each agent's context was compacted six or seven
times (34 in all), a little over two hours apart. Every compaction is followed by a message restating the party's position and
next step ("I'm checking the party's current progress and resuming the push toward Deadmines."), and no agent lost the
goal, forgot the party or repeated finished work afterwards. A likely reason is that all five, unprompted, kept a
notes file in the workspace, rewritten 38 to 55 times over the run, with the current state, gear, agreed plan and the
rules learned so far.

### Leadership and roles

Aldous led throughout: 1,700 party messages, 680 of the 1,511 whispers received, and the decisions to abandon the
outdoor mine and to take a training break. Aldous also took half of the damage taken inside the instance. Isolde did
70% of the healing; Corbin, specced for damage, did most of the rest and also dealt the most damage of anyone.
Thessaly cast Polymorph 284 times.

### SDK bugs

The most significant was in the global cooldown. The SDK restarted it at the end of each cast as well as at the
start, so casters waited about 1.5 seconds longer between spells than the server required. Aldous noticed the
symptom, Isolde found the line that restarts the timer on the server's spell-launch packet (which, for a spell with a
cast time, arrives when the cast finishes), and the priest, mage and paladin each patched around it in their own
scripts:

<AgentSay at="13:02:17" who="Thessaly" cls="mage">The logs confirm an extra cooldown wait after completed casts. I'm
removing that client-side delay for Frostbolt, Fireball, and Polymorph while keeping the normal global cooldown and
server validation.</AgentSay>

They found and worked around four more:

- The SDK picked the wrong Opening spell for some locks (the Furlbrow wardrobe, the gunpowder chest).
- Using an item couldn't target a game object (the Deadmines cannon).
- A function for the current stance always returned 0.
- The item search only found items whose name matched exactly.

### Cost

The run cost about $307 at list prices. Corbin cost $77, against $55 to $59 for each of the others, over the same seventeen hours and goal. The difference
comes from how Corbin's agent listened: it subscribed to party chat, and the party's scripts woke it 224 times. That
accounts for about 800 extra model calls and most of the gap between Corbin's 4,074 calls and the mage's 2,601.

### Data caveats

The charts were produced after the run from its own files (the judge's samples every 15 seconds, each character's
game events, the client recordings and the agents' Codex transcripts), which the harness wasn't yet designed to chart.
Known issues:

- The judge counted 115 deaths; the game events have 117, because the client reported a death when Aldous reclaimed
  a corpse and when Merrin logged back in already dead. The charts of killers and places use the events.
- The level chart marks a wipe at three or more deaths within two minutes, which also catches partial wipes and the
  corpse-run deaths after one. Counting only the times all five were dead at once gives 14.
- The judge's clock leaves out six pauses (about 11 minutes); the charts are in wall-clock time.
- Cost over time is priced per request at list rates and scaled to the harness's total, so the shape is reliable but
  individual points are estimates.
- The judge's own score was 2.1 for every agent, from a formula built for levelling runs (goals are now scored by
  time to the goal), so none of these charts use it.

## What's next

The run produced 47 findings: SDK defects found by the agents, harness failures and gaps in the scoring. The SDK now
exposes the state the agents had to extract from raw packets (every creature in combat with the party, crowd-control
auras on a unit, door and chest state). It still has no pull helper or crowd-control lock; those remain the agents'
job.

No other model has cleared the level-1 version yet. At a few hundred dollars and most of a day per run, it's too
expensive to repeat for every model, so the regular tasks are shorter (see <a href={sitePath('/results')}>results</a>).
The Deadmines task now starts at level 20 with a kit and records each boss as a checkpoint. The level-1 run is kept as
a reference.
